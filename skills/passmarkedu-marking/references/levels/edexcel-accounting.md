# Levels: Edexcel IAL Accounting (WAC11, WAC12)

Levels-based items are always **Evaluate**: 12 marks (4 levels: 1–3 / 4–6 / 7–9 / 10–12) in Section A, 6 marks (3 levels: 1–2 / 3–4 / 5–6) in Section B. The whole answer gets one level. Sources: Examiner Reports WAC11/WAC12 January and June 2025.

- **Both sides, then a decision.** One-sided answers stay low ("They could have attained a higher level … by not presenting only one-sided arguments", WAC11 Jun25 p.4); on a 6-mark item a valid conclusion is what separates Level 2 from Level 3 (WAC11 Jan25 p.5). Where the question offers options, the decision must choose one of them, ideally with a reason (WAC12 Jan25 p.6); give recommendations when the question asks for them.
- **Use the earlier calculations.** Restating in words the figures from earlier parts is Level 1–2 at most; explaining why the figures are what they are, adding a ratio, or quoting figures in support moves the answer up (WAC12 Jan25 pp.3–4). The candidate's own earlier figures count (own figure rule applies to the evaluation, WAC12 Jan25 p.4). Non-financial points from the scenario strengthen top-level answers (WAC12 Jun25 p.3).
- **Key condition of the question.** Answers that miss the condition the question turns on (book value rather than market value, whose point of view, only one of two options, drifting into "sources of finance" when the question is about the owner's statement) are held in the lower levels (WAC11 Jun25 p.4; WAC12 Jun25 pp.5–6).
- **Little use of the scenario:** the reports do not say whether this drops a level. Follow the question's own mark-scheme note (usually "generic answer → lowest mark in the level"); otherwise choose the level on balance, argument development and decision, and lower the mark within it.
- **What decides the level (examiner reports + level descriptors):**
  - Balance first: 6-mark items — a one-sided answer is Level 1 ("Very few candidates were able to achieve level 2 … Most of them presented one-sided arguments", WAC11 Jun25 p.8); 12-mark items — one-sided is Level 2 at most.
  - Then breadth and the decision: several relevant points on **both** sides plus a decision with a reason is Level 3; wide-ranging points on both sides (about five or more each) with a supported decision is Level 4 — even when the scenario's specific details are used lightly (that lowers the mark within the level, not the level).
  - Held at Level 2 (12 marks): a point that contradicts the case, ignoring the figures when the question turns on them (costs, ratios, the earlier calculations), or only restating earlier calculations in words (WAC12 Jan25 p.4).
  - Restating figures or facts from the question without saying what they mean for the decision is not a point; an answer made only of that earns 0.
- **No decision → bottom of the level.** An answer without a decision (or recommendation, where asked) takes the lowest mark of its level; an answer of unclear, undeveloped points with no decision earns 0–1.
- Within the level: start in the middle; move up for developed points tied to the figures and a supported decision, down for thin development or a weak decision.

Calibration pattern (from examiner-marked scripts; never name a paper here — a named example leaks the examiner's mark into blind tests): a 12-mark answer that sets out several relevant benefits and drawbacks of the policy and ends with a recommendation reached Level 4 from the examiner even though it barely used the scenario's specific details; the AI had placed it in Level 2 for being "generic". Light use of the scenario lowers the mark within the level, not the level.
